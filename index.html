An Artist of Abstract Expressionism

Sumiye Okoshi was among the first Japanese American women artists to arrive in New York after World War II. Her personal life is a story of living at opposite ends of different spectra: economic, social, and political - such views of the world she could not communicate through representational art, and from the beginning was led to abstract expressionism. Born in Seattle, Washington, her entrepreneur father took the family back to Japan when she was an infant. Although she grew up a child of new-money privilege, World War impoverished her family and left her a young widow.

Okoshi used her birthright American citizenship to return to the US, working as a maid for five years in Seattle while studying art at night in the few hours her employers spared her. After elite schooling in Tokyo, she had been married to an Asahi Shimbun newspaper reporter, Toshiyaki Okoshi; in wartime service to the Japanese Imperial Army, he died shortly after his return to Tokyo. In New York City she married a Japanese American of immigrant homesteading parents, a combat veteran of the segregated US Army 442nd Regiment of the same world war, and a painter of realism. Her close friends in New York included some of the first high-ranking Japanese women professionals in their fields with whom she had been acquainted through their private school upbringings in Japan, while she always maintained an open home in New York to many struggling artists.


The Greenwich Village Years 1955-2006

This website offers a sampling of Okoshi's paintings during her Greenwich Village years, from 1955 to about 2000, when she stopped painting due to ill health. Despite intense discrimination against Asian Americans in the US as being the ' dirty Japs' of WWII and 'Commy Chinks' of the 1950s' McCarthyism, a small number of Japanese artists, dancers, and musicians still petitioned to immigrate to the US, and found New York artists to be curious and welcoming, just as European impressionists of the 1800's had been influenced by Japanese traditional painting and art.
